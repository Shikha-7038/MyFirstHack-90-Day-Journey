# Day 87 — Cybersecurity Career Paths

## 1. Introduction

Cybersecurity is a broad field with multiple career paths. Different professionals work together to protect systems, investigate threats, test vulnerabilities, and manage security risks.

The three major areas covered today are:
- **Blue Team:** Defensive security
- **Red Team:** Offensive security and authorised testing
- **GRC:** Governance, Risk, and Compliance

## 2. Blue Team — Defensive Security

The Blue Team protects an organisation’s systems, networks, applications, and data. Its work includes monitoring security activity, detecting suspicious behaviour, investigating alerts, and responding to incidents.

### Common roles

**SOC Analyst (Security Operations Center Analyst)**
- Monitors security alerts and system logs.
- Investigates suspicious logins and unusual network activity.
- Determines whether an alert could indicate a security threat.
- Escalates confirmed or complex incidents when necessary.

**Incident Responder**
- Investigates and manages security incidents.
- Helps contain threats and limit damage.
- Supports recovery and documents findings.
- Helps prevent similar incidents from happening again.

**Threat Analyst**
- Studies attacker behaviour, tactics, and techniques.
- Analyses threat intelligence and suspicious activity.
- Helps organisations understand emerging threats.

**Security Engineer**
- Implements and maintains security tools and controls.
- Configures technologies such as firewalls, endpoint security, and monitoring systems.
- Improves the organisation’s security infrastructure.

**Security Architect**
- Designs the overall security structure of systems and networks.
- Plans how security controls work together.
- Considers security during system design.

## 3. Red Team — Offensive Security

The Red Team evaluates security by simulating attacker techniques in an authorised, controlled manner. The goal is to identify weaknesses before malicious attackers exploit them. Testing must have permission and a defined scope.

### Common roles

**Penetration Tester**
- Tests systems, applications, and networks for vulnerabilities.
- Uses approved techniques to identify weaknesses.
- Documents findings and recommends fixes.

**Red Team Operator**
- Simulates realistic attack scenarios within an authorised engagement.
- Tests how effectively security controls detect and respond to attacks.
- Helps identify gaps in defensive capabilities.

**Vulnerability Researcher**
- Investigates software, systems, and applications for security flaws.
- Analyses how vulnerabilities occur and may be exploited.
- Helps teams understand and fix weaknesses.

Red Team work can require skills in networking, operating systems, scripting, application security, and vulnerability analysis. Some specialised roles require substantial experience, though junior opportunities also exist.

## 4. GRC — Governance, Risk, and Compliance

GRC focuses on managing cybersecurity at the organisational level. It connects security practices with business objectives, policies, risk decisions, and applicable requirements.

### Common roles

**GRC Analyst**
- Helps develop and maintain security policies.
- Supports risk assessments and compliance activities.
- Tracks requirements and control implementation.

**Risk Analyst**
- Identifies potential threats to organisational assets.
- Evaluates risks based on likelihood and potential impact.
- Helps prioritise risks and recommends controls.

**Security Auditor**
- Reviews security controls, processes, and documentation.
- Checks whether controls meet applicable requirements and standards.
- Records findings and identifies areas for improvement.

GRC roles often require analytical thinking, attention to detail, documentation, communication, and knowledge of security frameworks, risk management, and compliance requirements.

## 5. Comparing the Three Areas

| Area | Main purpose | Typical activities |
|---|---|---|
| Blue Team | Defend systems | Monitor alerts, investigate threats, respond to incidents |
| Red Team | Test defences | Conduct authorised security testing and identify vulnerabilities |
| GRC | Manage organisational security | Assess risks, maintain policies, support audits and compliance |

The three areas have different day-to-day responsibilities, but all contribute to protecting an organisation’s systems, information, and operations.

## 6. Skills Used Across Cybersecurity

- **Technical knowledge:** Understanding networks, operating systems, applications, and security controls.
- **Analytical thinking:** Examining evidence, identifying patterns, and evaluating explanations.
- **Problem-solving:** Finding practical ways to address vulnerabilities, threats, and security gaps.
- **Communication:** Explaining findings to technical teams and business stakeholders.
- **Documentation:** Recording investigations, test results, risks, and recommendations accurately.
- **Continuous learning:** Keeping up with changing threats, tools, and security practices.

The importance of each skill varies by role. A SOC Analyst may spend considerable time investigating logs and alerts, while a GRC Analyst may focus more on risk assessments, policies, and audit evidence.

## 7. Entry-Level Opportunities and Career Development

Possible entry points include:
- Junior SOC Analyst
- Security Operations Intern
- Junior Security Analyst
- Junior GRC Analyst
- Junior Penetration Tester, where suitable opportunities are available

One possible defensive-security progression is:

**SOC Analyst L1 → SOC Analyst L2 → Incident Responder, Threat Analyst, or another security specialisation**

This is not a fixed career ladder. People may move between areas as they gain experience and develop new skills.

Foundational knowledge in networking, operating systems, security logs, threat analysis, and incident response can support defensive roles. Practical labs, documented investigations, and clear technical reports can help demonstrate applied knowledge.

## 8. Task — Map Your Path into the Field

### Objective
Identify cybersecurity career areas, connect relevant skills to roles, and outline a realistic first step into the field.

### Career areas
- **Blue Team:** Defending systems and responding to threats.
- **Red Team:** Testing security with authorisation to find weaknesses.
- **GRC:** Managing organisational risk, policies, and compliance.

### Role and topic mapping

| Role | Relevant knowledge or skills |
|---|---|
| SOC Analyst | Security logs, alerts, networking, threat frameworks, and risk prioritisation |
| Incident Responder | Incident-response phases, evidence handling, containment, and recovery |
| Penetration Tester | Networking, vulnerabilities, authorised testing, and attack techniques |
| GRC Analyst | Compliance, security policies, risk assessment, and audit documentation |

### Career reflection
Consider which area currently fits your interests and strengths. You do not have to select a permanent specialisation immediately.

### First-step plan
1. Choose one or two realistic entry-level roles to target.
2. Review the skills and tools commonly requested for those roles.
3. Practise through authorised labs and practical exercises.
4. Document genuine work with the evidence, tools, findings, and recommendations.
5. Prepare a relevant CV and professional profile.
6. Use job descriptions and feedback to identify the next skills to develop.

## 9. Key Takeaways

- Cybersecurity is a broad field, not a single job.
- Blue Team professionals focus on defence, detection, investigation, and response.
- Red Team professionals conduct authorised testing to identify security weaknesses.
- GRC professionals help manage risks, policies, and compliance.
- The three areas contribute to the same goal but require different day-to-day skills.
- SOC Analyst and other junior security roles can be entry points, depending on the employer and candidate preparation.
- A career path can change as practical experience and interests develop.
- Practical work and clear documentation help show how cybersecurity knowledge is applied.
