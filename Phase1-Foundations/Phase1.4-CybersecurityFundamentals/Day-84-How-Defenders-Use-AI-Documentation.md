# Day 84 — How Defenders Use AI

## Overview

**Topic:** How AI supports defensive cybersecurity  
**Task:** Reason about AI on defence  
**Format:** Thinking and writing; no terminal commands

Day 84 completes the AI topic by exploring how defenders use AI and why human judgement remains essential. Together with Day 83, it presents a balanced view: AI can amplify both attacks and defences, but it does not replace cybersecurity fundamentals or the human role.

## Notes

### 1. AI assists; it does not replace

AI can help defenders monitor systems, detect suspicious activity, analyse alerts, investigate incidents, and support responses. It helps security teams work more efficiently, particularly when they face more data than they can realistically inspect manually.

AI is not infallible. It can produce **false positives** (flagging harmless activity as suspicious) and **miss real threats**. It may also lack the full business and operational context needed to understand what an alert means. Human analysts remain responsible for interpreting evidence, judging severity, deciding on appropriate action, and maintaining oversight and accountability.

### 2. Finding the needle in the haystack

Security systems produce large volumes of logs, network traffic, events, and alerts. A genuine threat can be hidden among ordinary activity.

AI can sift through this data, recognise patterns, and highlight unusual behaviour for further investigation.

**Anomaly detection** is the process of identifying activity that differs from an established pattern of normal behaviour. Examples include an unexpected login or an unusual data transfer.

This connects to the investigative skill practised in Days 57–59: **spotting what doesn't belong**. An unusual event is not automatically malicious. It is a signal to examine the evidence and context.

### 3. How AI amplifies defensive work

1. **Faster investigation and analysis:** AI can gather related information, summarise findings, and help analysts examine suspicious activity more quickly.
2. **Routine response support:** AI-assisted systems can help automate predefined actions, such as isolating a device when there is strong evidence of compromise. Appropriate safeguards and oversight are important.
3. **Reducing alert fatigue:** AI can group related alerts, filter routine activity, and prioritise potentially serious events so analysts can focus on what matters.
4. **Supporting less-experienced analysts:** AI can explain unfamiliar terminology, suggest possible explanations, and point to evidence that needs checking. Its suggestions still require verification.

### 4. Why the analyst remains essential

AI does not reliably make every decision correctly. It can generate false positives, miss genuine threats, and misunderstand the situation because it lacks complete context. Decisions about whether an incident is real, how serious it is, and what response is appropriate require human judgement, accountability, and oversight.

This is encouraging for people entering cybersecurity. Skills such as understanding attacks, investigating evidence, analysing activity, assessing risk, and choosing responses remain important. Security professionals need to learn how to work with AI tools, interpret their output, and question results when the evidence does not fit.

### 5. The balanced picture: attackers and defenders

| AI used by attackers — Day 83 | AI used by defenders — Day 84 |
|---|---|
| Makes phishing more convincing and personalised. | Helps identify unusual behaviour and suspicious activity. |
| Speeds up reconnaissance and analysis of public information. | Helps sift through large volumes of logs, events, and alerts. |
| Supports realistic impersonation and scalable attempts. | Accelerates investigations and helps prioritise alerts. |
| Amplifies existing attack methods. | Amplifies existing defensive capabilities. |

AI strengthens both sides, but neither side succeeds simply by using it. Cybersecurity knowledge and human judgement remain essential.

## Day 84 Task — Reason About AI on Defence

### Step 1: State the core framing

AI helps defenders by amplifying existing security work, including monitoring systems, detecting suspicious activity, analysing alerts, and responding to incidents. It helps security teams process information faster and work more efficiently rather than replacing human analysts.

The main problem is the overwhelming volume of data generated by systems, networks, logs, and security alerts. Manually examining everything is difficult, so AI helps defenders identify and prioritise activity that deserves attention.

### Step 2: Explain the needle in the haystack

Defenders must find potentially malicious activity hidden among enormous amounts of normal system activity. AI can analyse large volumes of data, recognise patterns, and highlight unusual behaviour that might otherwise be overlooked.

**Anomaly detection** means identifying activity that deviates from an established pattern of normal behaviour. Examples include an unusual login, an unexpected data transfer, or activity that does not match typical system usage.

This connects to the investigative skills developed in Days 57–59: spotting what doesn't belong. AI can help identify suspicious differences, but an anomaly is not automatically an attack. A human analyst must investigate the surrounding evidence and determine whether the activity is genuinely suspicious.

### Step 3: List the defensive amplifications

1. **Accelerating investigation and analysis:** AI can gather related information, summarise findings, and help analysts understand suspicious activity more quickly.
2. **Automating routine responses:** AI-assisted security systems can automate predefined actions, such as isolating a device when strong evidence indicates compromise, subject to appropriate safeguards.
3. **Reducing alert fatigue:** AI can group related alerts, filter routine activity, and prioritise potentially serious events so analysts can focus on the most important issues.
4. **Supporting less-experienced analysts:** AI can explain unfamiliar terminology, suggest possible explanations, and help analysts identify evidence they should examine, while their findings still require verification.

These capabilities help security teams handle large workloads without removing the need for investigation and oversight.

### Step 4: Argue why AI doesn't replace the analyst

AI is unlikely to eliminate the need for security analysts because it can produce false positives, miss genuine threats, and lack the full business and operational context needed to interpret an alert correctly. Decisions about whether an incident is genuine, how serious it is, and what response is appropriate require human judgement, accountability, and oversight.

This is encouraging for someone entering cybersecurity because technical fundamentals remain valuable. Understanding attacks, investigating evidence, analysing risks, and making informed decisions are essential skills for working effectively with AI-assisted security tools. AI changes how analysts work, but it does not remove the need to understand cybersecurity.

### Step 5: See both sides together

Attackers use AI to make attacks more convincing, faster, more scalable, and easier to carry out, while defenders use AI to analyse large volumes of data, identify suspicious activity, accelerate investigations, and improve responses. AI amplifies both offence and defence, but it does not replace cybersecurity fundamentals or human judgement. Skilled security professionals remain essential for interpreting evidence, making critical decisions, and using AI responsibly.

## Key Takeaways

- AI amplifies defensive capabilities rather than replacing security work.
- Large volumes of logs, traffic, events, and alerts make automated analysis valuable.
- Anomaly detection identifies activity that differs from normal patterns; unusual does not automatically mean malicious.
- AI can accelerate investigations, support routine responses, reduce alert fatigue, and help analysts learn.
- AI can make mistakes and may lack the context needed to interpret every event correctly.
- Human judgement, accountability, verification, and cybersecurity fundamentals remain essential.
- A balanced understanding considers how attackers and defenders both use AI.
