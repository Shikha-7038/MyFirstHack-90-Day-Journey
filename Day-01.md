# Objective

---

# Understanding Data Breaches

Before starting today's lesson, I thought a data breach simply meant that someone's account had been hacked.

After completing the lesson, I learned that a data breach is much broader.

A data breach occurs when unauthorized individuals gain access to sensitive information stored by an organization. This information may include:

- Email addresses
- Passwords
- Phone numbers
- Dates of birth
- Physical addresses
- Financial information
- Other personal data

I also understood that the breach usually happens to a company or service rather than directly to individual users. However, the exposed information can later be used to target those users.

---

# Why Data Breaches Matter

One of the most important lessons from today was realizing that a data breach is not simply about leaked information.

Once personal information becomes publicly available, attackers may use it for:

- Credential stuffing attacks
- Phishing emails
- Identity theft
- Social engineering
- Account takeover attempts

This helped me understand why cybersecurity professionals monitor breaches even when the breach does not directly involve them.

---

# Practical Activity – Checking My Email

The practical task for Day 1 was to check my primary email address using **Have I Been Pwned**, a website that maintains a database of publicly known data breaches.

Instead of reading about data breaches, I applied the concept to my own digital life.

## My Finding

After searching my primary email address:

- **No public data breaches were found.**

Although this was reassuring, I also learned an important point.

A result showing **no breaches** does **not** guarantee that an email account has never been compromised. It simply means that the email address has not appeared in the public breach datasets currently tracked by Have I Been Pwned.

This helped me understand the limitations of security tools and why results should always be interpreted carefully.

---

# Understanding Have I Been Pwned

Before using the website, I assumed it somehow hacked accounts to determine whether an email had been exposed.

After learning more about the service, I discovered that it works very differently.

Have I Been Pwned collects information from publicly known breach databases and allows users to check whether their email addresses appear within those datasets.

The website never attempts to log into an account or verify passwords.

Instead, it compares the email entered by the user against known breach records.

Understanding this process gave me greater confidence in using the service.

Today's lesson made me realize that cybersecurity starts with awareness.

Checking my own email helped me understand that security should be based on evidence rather than assumptions.

Even though my email was not found in any known breach, I recognized that good security habits are still necessary because new breaches occur regularly.

# Challenges I Faced

Since this was my first practical cybersecurity activity, I had several questions while completing the exercise.

Initially, I wondered whether seeing **"No pwnage found"** meant my email was completely secure forever.

After learning more, I understood that:

- It only means no known public breaches currently include my email.
- Future breaches could still occur.
- Security practices such as strong passwords and multi-factor authentication remain essential.

I also learned that security tools provide useful information, but they should not create a false sense of complete safety.

# Key Takeaways

- A data breach occurs when unauthorized individuals gain access to sensitive information stored by an organization.
- Personal information exposed during breaches can be used in phishing, credential stuffing, identity theft, and other cyberattacks.
- Have I Been Pwned allows users to check whether their email appears in known public breach databases.
